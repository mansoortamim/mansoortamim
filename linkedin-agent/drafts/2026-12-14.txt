"It's only a minor change" is the most expensive sentence in a GMP facility.

A new supplier for the same grade of excipient. A different gasket on the same machine. One column moved on a form. A software update "with no functional impact".

Each of these can be logged as minor and come back months later as a deviation.

The classification isn't the problem. The missing question is: what else does this touch?
- Which SOPs, BMRs and forms reference it?
- Does any validation or qualification rely on the old state?
- Does a regulatory filing describe it?
- Who needs training before the change goes live, not after?

A change control that answers those four is rarely the one that hurts you.

The one that hurts you was closed in a day because everyone agreed it was small.

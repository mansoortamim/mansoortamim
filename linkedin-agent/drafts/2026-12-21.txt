A risk score one point below the action threshold isn't a low risk. It's a negotiated one.

Anyone who has sat in a QRM review knows the moment. The RPN lands just above the line, someone says "detection is probably better than that", and the number drops.

Sometimes that's right. Often it's the meeting wanting to finish.

What keeps an FMEA honest:
- Score severity first, and never lower it because of controls. Controls change occurrence and detection, not how bad the failure is.
- Write down the evidence behind every occurrence and detection score. "Operator experience" isn't evidence.
- Challenge any score that sits just under the threshold. Ask what would have to be true for it to be one point higher.

A risk assessment isn't there to prove the process is safe. It's there to find where it isn't.

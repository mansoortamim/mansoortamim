If the logbook gets filled in at the end of the shift, the data is already compromised.

It might be accurate. It might match the batch. But it isn't contemporaneous, and an inspector will read it that way.

ALCOA+ isn't paperwork for its own sake. Each letter is a question an auditor will ask:
- Attributable: who did it?
- Legible: can someone else read it?
- Contemporaneous: was it recorded when it happened?
- Original: is this the first record, or a copy of a note on a glove?
- Accurate: does it match what actually happened?

The "+" adds complete, consistent, enduring and available.

Most data integrity findings don't start with fraud. They start with convenience: a rough note, a time written from memory, a correction with no reason given.

Fix the convenience and most findings go away. Put the logbook where the work is. Give the form a field for every value. Make a proper correction quicker than a hidden one.
